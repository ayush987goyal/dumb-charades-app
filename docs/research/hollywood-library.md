# Hollywood movies for Dumb Charades: research and curated catalog

Scope: a charades-optimised Hollywood list for Indian friend groups (urban, 20s to 40s) who know Bollywood best and Hollywood second. This replaces the 81-title list in `lib/movies/hollywood.ts`, which leaned on prestige films that are hard to mime. Final list: **440 titles** (see Section 6). Research date: 24 September 2026.

## 2. Selection rules

The Bollywood catalog (`docs/bollywood-charades-movies-research.md`) uses three filters: concrete multi-word titles, strict gatekeeping on single words, and explicit exclusions. The Hollywood list applies the same logic, adjusted for an audience that knows these films second-hand.

**Rule 1. Recognition first.** A title qualifies only if a typical urban Indian player in their 20s to 40s has likely seen it, seen its poster, or grown up with it on TV (Star Movies, HBO, Cartoon Network, Disney Channel) or OTT (Netflix, Prime, JioHotstar). Evidence: worldwide or India box office, community charades lists, or long TV/OTT life. Films known mostly to cinephiles (Zodiac, Moonlight, Her) fail even if critically loved.

**Rule 2. Word-by-word decomposition.** Prefer titles made of concrete, mimeable English words: animals (Lion, Shark, Panda, Wolf, Dragon), objects (Ring, Mask, Car, Hook), people and roles (King, Princess, Boss, Baby, Godfather), places (Home, Island, Street, Museum), actions (Run, Fight, Catch, Kill, Die), numbers and colours. Examples: Home Alone, The Lion King, Finding Nemo, Die Hard, Night at the Museum, Catch Me If You Can, How to Train Your Dragon, The Wolf of Wall Street.

**Rule 3. Single words must be concrete or carry a signature mime.** Allowed: concrete nouns or verbs (Jaws, Titanic, Frozen, Cars, Up, Speed, Saw, Hook, Ghost, Taken, Split, Smile, Weapons, Lion, Gravity), or an unmistakable character pose that the audience knows (Joker stair dance, Rocky fists on the steps, Rambo headband and gun, Shrek ogre roar, Michael moonwalk, Ratatouille rat chef, Tarzan chest-beat and vine swing, Pinocchio growing nose). Rejected: abstract words (Her, Arrival, Prisoners, Memento, Soul, Tenet, Us, Nope) and proper nouns with no hook (Logan, Zodiac, Mulan, Luca, Encanto).

**Rule 4. One entry per franchise, with exceptions.** Use the base title (Toy Story, Despicable Me, Shrek, Jurassic Park, Mission Impossible). A sequel gets its own slot only if its subtitle is iconic and adds new mimeable words or the audience knows it as a distinct film: Avengers Infinity War, Avengers Endgame, Captain America Civil War, Spider-Man No Way Home, Spider-Man Brand New Day, Avatar The Way of Water, Top Gun Maverick, Deadpool and Wolverine, and five Harry Potter titles whose subtitles are strong charades material (Stone, Chamber, Prisoner, Goblet of Fire, Deathly Hallows). Plain numbered sequels are always folded into the base (no Inside Out 2, Toy Story 5, Frozen 2, Zootopia 2).

**Rule 5. Exclusions.** Bare numbers or stylised digits (1917, 2012, 300, Se7en, Blade Runner 2049), misspellings and profanity (Inglourious Basterds), long abstract titles (Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind, Everything Everywhere All at Once, No Country for Old Men, There Will Be Blood), non-Hollywood foreign-language films (Parasite, Ne Zha 2, Train to Busan), and 2026 releases that underperformed or have not released yet (The Mandalorian and Grogu, Supergirl, Avengers Doomsday due December 2026). One exception is kept on purpose: F1 (2025) is a letter and number, but the racing-car mime is immediate.

**Formatting.** ASCII only. Colons dropped, matching the existing file style (Avengers Endgame, Mad Max Fury Road). Subtitles kept only when they identify the film or add mimeable words. Trailing periods removed (Monsters Inc, Mrs Doubtfire, The Super Mario Bros Movie). Hyphens and apostrophes kept as in the official title (Spider-Man, WALL-E, A Bug's Life).

## 5. Notes and caveats

- **Bollywood collision:** Wanted (2008, Angelina Jolie) shares its title with Wanted (2009, Salman Khan) in the Bollywood list. Fine if the app keeps Hollywood and Bollywood decks separate; drop it if decks can be mixed.
- **Remakes share a title:** The Jungle Book, The Lion King, Aladdin, Beauty and the Beast, Cinderella, The Little Mermaid, Lilo and Stitch, Moana, Dumbo and How to Train Your Dragon each cover both the animated and live-action versions. One entry each.
- **Hardest titles in the list** (kept for recognition, but the first to cut if playtests show dead rounds): The Shawshank Redemption, Pulp Fiction, The Curious Case of Benjamin Button, The Pursuit of Happyness, Fantastic Beasts and Where to Find Them, Crouching Tiger Hidden Dragon, Harry Potter and the Prisoner of Azkaban, To All the Boys I've Loved Before, It.
- **Not British-only, but close:** Slumdog Millionaire and Bend It Like Beckham are UK productions with US distribution. Kept because they are among the most recognised English-language films in India.
- **Box office figures for 2026** were still moving at research time. The Odyssey, Michael and Project Hail Mary were included on the basis of worldwide totals above 650 million USD by September 2026.
